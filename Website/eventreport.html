How to Use Spatial Data to Create a Wildfire Index

   For this assignment, I viewed a web seminar presented by CARTO, a company that provides a spatial analysis platform with the same name. The seminar is titled “How to Use Spatial Data to Create a Wildfire Risk Index” and is presented by Lucia Garcia-Duarte and Mark Gibbas. Gibbas, the CEO of Weather Source, discusses the premise of and need for a Wildfire Risk Index (WRI) and Garcia-Duarte, a data scientist at CARTO, discusses the technical details of the creation of the index. Although I don’t have a specific desire to work in a meteorology-related profession, the WRI is particularly interesting to me because the analysis done to create it is specifically the kind of work I would like to do – deep analyzation of data to find unseen patterns for the purpose of fulfilling real-world needs.

   The seminar begins with Mark Gibbas stating the need for a platform for measuring wildfire risk by discussing the increase in the risk of wildfires in recent years, particularly on the west side of the United States and other parts of the world. The purpose of the Wildfire Risk Index is to be able to analyze levels of wildfire risk using spatial data. Gibbas talks about the factors that contribute to the cause of wildfires and how climate change is creating a greater risk of wildfires. Organizations like government agencies, insurance companies, power and utilities, and landscape managers need data analysis, in this case, particularly spatial data analysis, in order to answer pressing questions and deal with issues regarding wildfire risk. After this introduction, Lucia Garcia-Duarte goes into great detail discussing the creation of the Wildfire Risk Index.

   Combining weather data provided by Weather Source with sociodemographic and geographic data from other sources, Garcia-Duarte was able to perform data analysis and create visualizations relevant to the recent increase in wildfires. The final result is an interactive platform that allows users to analyze wildfire risks across different regions during various times of the year. The product shown in the seminar focused solely on California. Garcia-Duarte goes into great detail on the construction of the index by breaking it into three steps: clustering the data by selecting the type of land prone to wildfire, selecting fire precursors by finding variables related to burned areas, and defining the index by combining those selected variables. To conclude, she shows an example of a use case by discussing how the WRI can be used to improve home insurance. A portion of the seminar I found particularly interesting was how she used critical thinking to elevate the accuracy of the data analysis. Areas in California designated as trees or shrubs were at noticeably higher risk of wildfire compared to other areas and so the analysis focused on these areas. The data source this information was provided by marked desert areas as shrub, which created some inconsistencies in the variable as desert areas are at lower risk due to their lack of vegetation. In order to create more consistency within the shrub variable, she had to reclassify desert areas so that the wildfire risk of other shrub areas was not being diluted by low-risk desert regions.

   This seminar is relevant to my career goals and information studies because the type of data analysis performed to create the WRI is part of what I’ve been learning to do in school over the last couple of years. Data analysis and the creation of visualizations using data has been a large part of my education and a core concept of many of the courses from the concentration I selected as part of my Information Science major– data science and analytics. While I am currently not set on a career path, the tools and processes used in creating the WRI are part of a skillset that I am currently developing on my journey to becoming an information professional. Watching and learning from this seminar, which was partially produced by a data scientist, has given me affirmation that the knowledge I have been acquiring will be valuable in my future.
