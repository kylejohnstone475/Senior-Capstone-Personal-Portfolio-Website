#GEA1 Assignment: Ethical Case Study Analysis

The "Internet of Things" (IOT)

   Technology is constantly transforming and evolving. Advancements in technology can open doors to new opportunities that could have previously been thought of as unrealistic or impossible. The internet is a marvel of human engineering and intelligence and has spawned the "Internet of Things" or IOT. The IOT is a concept of a system that incorporates a network of technologies and data collecting devices that can communicate across the internet and are heavily incorporated into the daily lives of people around the world through physical infrastructure or wearable technology. Just about any device that is part of a broader system can be considered an IOT device, such as smartwatches, smart thermostats, or security devices. This technology system has changed the way humans think, work, and live their lives (FwThinking 2013). Although the IOT can make life easier and further help advance technology, it raises ethical concerns regarding privacy and security. This case study will explore ethical and policy implications regarding the IOT, and how the IOT relates to my career.

   The IOT is comprised of devices that record and collect data. The data that is collected can often be used to the benefit of the consumer, but that information can be personal and private. While consumers can relinquish some of their private information to organizations and companies for their benefit, those organizations have a duty to communicate what the information will be used for and to preserve the consumers' privacy beyond that (Singer & Perry 2015). In the United States, this concept is currently part of the law. Current privacy policies stipulate that companies will not share personally identifiable information with third parties when it comes to wearable IOT devices. However, if the service of the device demands the need for sharing of personal information, the company must disclose the need for sharing (Singer & Perry 2015). Beyond these restrictions, it is unclear whether the government itself is willing to maintain individual privacy. James Clapper, a previous Director of National Intelligence, has stated that "intelligence services might use the IOT for identification, surveillance, monitoring, location tracking, and targeting for recruitment, or to gain access to networks or user credentials" (Karsten 2016). While some of these surveillance methods may be necessary for certain police work, their general use could encroach on privacy rights.

   Outside of privacy, security is also a growing concern. Many IOT devices that consumers use have the ability to quantify personal biological data. For instance, smart watches can track heart rate, sleeping pattern, etc. The biological data these devices will be able to monitor is only going to become more advanced and in-depth. If bad actors are able to breach security and access this information, the general public could be at risk. The 23andMe breach from a few years ago signify how biological information can be commodified for potentially malicious purposes, as at least some hacked data was found for sale on dark web forums (Coleman 2025).

   As an aspiring information professional, I can foresee the involvement of the IOT in my future career. Pursuing a career in data science, collecting and analyzing data will be a large part of my job. It is possible that I will acquire a job that involves analyzing data collected from IOT technology. The IOT is just about everywhere, collecting vast amounts of data that can be mined for useful patterns and relationships that can aid a business or organization.

   To conclude, the "Internet of Things"is a growing and significant technology concept that has ethical and policy implications and it may potentially be a large part of my future career. The ethical implications regarding the IOT stem from privacy and security concerns. Policy regarding the IOT revolves around these important subjects, which the future of remains worrisome for those that value privacy or are concerned about the security of their information. The IOT is a byproduct of the marvel of technology that is the internet, and has the potential to significantly improve our lives, but it must be managed carefully.
