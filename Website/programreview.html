BSIS Program Review

   When I transferred to USF two years ago, I was uncertain what direction I wanted to go in career-wise. While I have had interests, I have always struggled to land on something I could find passion for. Before transferring, I had spent several years at a community college dabbling in computer science courses, so I gravitated towards information science, particularly the data science and analytics concentration. Taking these courses, I was able to discover a new interest in working with large amounts of data and a form of programming that was more my speed. Now a few weeks from graduation, I am finally feeling confident about my career trajectory.

   The data science courses are where I found my passion for big data and R programming. The courses that revolve around these concepts and that I took the most from are:

  • Introduction to Data Science
  • Introduction to Visual Analytics
  • Introduction to Data & Text Mining
  • Predictive Analytics

   In these courses, I learned about concepts such as data mining, data visualization, and data science modeling. The knowledge I gained from these courses will ultimately be some of the most valuable concepts that I can take into a data science career. To my understanding, projects in data analysis typically revolve these concepts. These courses focused heavily on the R programming language, which is the primary tool I use for working with data, conducting data analysis, and creating visualizations.

   Three assignments that were useful for me as I prepare for a career in information-related organizations:

  • Introduction to Data & Text Mining: Final Project
  • Predictive Analytics: Final Project
  • Information Behaviors: Annotated Bibliography

   The Introduction to Data & Text Mining Final Project as well as the Predictive Analytics Final Project are very similar in scope and I feel that the work I’m doing on those projects are similar to a data science project in the real world. Both of the projects are group projects that focus on finding a dataset, conducting some form of analysis on the dataset, and creating a presentation on the findings. Having to collaborate with classmates and work with a deadline in mind feels like how a business or organization would approach a project. The Information Behaviors Annotated Bibliography assignment was one of the more challenging assignments I completed in my Information Science courses. The purpose of this assignment was to find several information-related scientific journals and to analyze and summarize them. The assignment allowed me to use all the skills and knowledge revolving around the concept of information I had acquired in my courses and apply them.

   Majoring in information science with a concentration in data science and analytics has been an enjoyable challenge that led me to find new skills, knowledge, and interests that I can take into my career. While I was uncertain which direction I wanted to go in when I first transferred to USF, I am now confident in my abilities and where I want my career to take me.
